Rating as of October 6, 2026: 7/10 · Pay Attention

No money is known to have been stolen, and the core banking systems seem to be fine. The attackers went after the less-watched systems around the edges.

What changed

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung said AI models appear to have been used in a recent wave of hacking attacks on banks. "In some hacking incidents, signs have emerged of AI being used, causing considerable public concern and anxiety," he said at a Cabinet meeting, and ordered officials to establish what happened quickly [1][2].

Here is what's known so far:

  • The scale. More than seven financial institutions reported breaches of customers' personal information, affecting over 68,000 people [1][3]. Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, Hana Bank and Woori Bank are among those named [2]. Bloomberg reports about 40,000 affected customers at Yegaram Savings Bank and 25,000 at Shinhan [4].
  • The targets. South Korea's top financial regulator said initial findings suggest some attacks went after "less closely monitored external websites and servers used by loan agents and employees" rather than core systems [4].
  • The AI tool. Traces of ARTEX, an open-source AI tool that automates finding and exploiting security weaknesses, were found at IP addresses used in the attacks, according to Yonhap [4].
  • What's unclear. Authorities say attribution remains unclear because the tool is publicly available and the IP addresses were spread across multiple countries [4]. They haven't said how much of the attack the AI carried out [2].

Police have opened a full-scale investigation, and regulators ordered the whole financial sector to run security inspections [2][4]. No customer funds are known to have been stolen.

Why it matters

Most of the AI-and-hacking stories so far have involved AI agents escaping tests, or a single company's report on a criminal campaign. This is a government, at the presidential level, saying AI appears to have been used in real attacks on its banking sector.

The lesson that stood out to me is a very unglamorous one. The attackers didn't need movie-style hacking powers. They went after the boring side systems that people forgot to lock as tightly as the main vault. AI is very good at checking every door, patiently and cheaply, and that's where the risk is.

What you can probably ignore

"AI robbed South Korean banks." Personal information was exposed. No customer money is known to have been taken.

"The AI picked its own targets." There's no evidence of that. Someone chose to attack these banks, and the AI's role is still being worked out [2][4].

Why it earned this rating

On the side of a higher number:

  • Real harm to tens of thousands of real people, whose names, phone numbers and income details could now be used for scams [1][3].
  • Multiple major financial institutions in one campaign [2].
  • A national government is treating it as an AI-assisted attack [1].

On the side of a lower number:

  • The AI's exact role hasn't been established [2][4].
  • Core banking systems appear to be intact and no funds are known to be stolen.
  • A human was behind it.

Credible real-world harm with AI apparently involved is a 7. If investigators confirm the AI did most of the work, this goes up.

What I'm watching next

  • The investigation's findings on how much of the attack the AI carried out.
  • What happens to ARTEX, which anyone could download.
  • Follow-on scams. The leaked data could fuel phishing aimed at the affected customers.

Bunker Check

Current rating: 7/10 · Pay Attention

Direction: Holding, leaning up

Could move higher if: Investigators confirm the AI ran most of the attack, or money is stolen using the leaked data.

The shovel stays in the garage. The side door, however, needs a better lock.
