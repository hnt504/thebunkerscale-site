**Welcome to The Bunker Scale.**

This whole thing started with a question.

I had been reading more and more about AI safety. Some of it sounded genuinely concerning. Some of it sounded wildly overblown. And some of it was so technical that by the time I finished reading, I knew considerably more words and somehow less about whether I should actually be worried.

Eventually I asked the question the only way that made sense:

**On a scale from 0 to “pack up your things and build an underground bunker,” how serious is this AI situation?**

It was partly a joke.

But only partly.

Because AI has reached an odd point where two things can be true at the same time.

There is an enormous amount of fear surrounding it.

And there are also some very real reasons to pay attention.

That combination makes it surprisingly difficult to figure out how concerned an ordinary person should be.

One headline says an AI system tried to deceive its developers. Another says a model found a cybersecurity vulnerability. Someone warns that AI could pose an existential threat. Someone else says those concerns are ridiculous. Governments start holding summits. AI companies introduce new safeguards. Another model comes out three weeks later that can do something the previous one couldn't.

Meanwhile, most of us are simply trying to figure out whether any of this changes our lives.

That is where **The Bunker Scale** comes in.

## Somewhere Between “Interesting” and “Start Digging”

I am not interested in pretending AI poses no risk.

I am equally uninterested in turning every unusual model behavior into evidence that Skynet has quietly opened a LinkedIn account.

What I want to know is much simpler:

**What actually happened?**

Was it a controlled experiment or something that happened in the real world? Did the AI make a suggestion, or did it actually take an action? Was a human directing it? Did anyone get hurt? Was the behavior independently confirmed? What did the researchers themselves say about the limitations? Did an AI company change its safeguards because of it? Did governments respond?

And perhaps most importantly, **did this development actually change what we know about the capabilities or risks of current AI systems?**

Those questions are considerably less exciting than **AI ESCAPES LAB**, but they tend to produce much more useful answers.

## Why a Rating?

Because sometimes you finish reading an article and still have no idea what you're supposed to do with the information.

So developments covered here get a number from **0 to 10**.

At the bottom of the scale is **Background Noise**. Interesting perhaps, but probably not something that changes the picture.

In the middle you'll find **Worth Watching** and **Eyebrow Raised**, where developments become more substantial and deserve closer attention.

At **7-8**, we reach **Pay Attention**. These are stories backed by credible evidence that suggest something meaningful may have changed.

A **9** is **Stock the Shelves**.

And a **10** is, naturally, **Get in the Bunker**.

For the record, I would be thrilled never to publish a 10.

I am also not literally recommending that anyone buy **seventeen years’ worth of canned goods** when something receives a 9.

The names are meant to make the scale memorable. **The ratings themselves are meant to be taken seriously.**

## What You'll Find Here

The Bunker Scale will focus mostly on developments involving AI safety and risk, particularly areas where the consequences could extend beyond an impressive new chatbot feature.

That includes autonomy, cybersecurity, biological risks, military applications, real-world AI harm, new safety measures from major AI companies, significant regulation, and evidence that current systems are behaving in ways their developers did not expect.

There will also be explainers.

Because occasionally the bigger question is more interesting than the headline.

What does it actually mean when researchers say an AI system behaved "autonomously"?

What's the difference between an AI saying something dangerous and actually being capable of doing it?

Why are governments talking about AI safety now?

What would genuine loss of control even look like?

And how much should we trust demonstrations created specifically to push AI systems until they fail?

Those questions deserve more than panic or dismissal.

## I'm Not Pretending to Have a Crystal Ball

This part matters.

The Bunker Scale is not an attempt to declare which AI researchers, companies, governments, optimists, pessimists, or people arguing with each other online are ultimately going to be proven right.

Nobody knows exactly where this technology is heading.

A rating is simply my assessment of the evidence available at the time.

That means ratings can change.

Something that initially looks alarming may become less concerning when researchers learn more about it. Something seemingly minor may become much more important when other teams reproduce it or when it begins happening outside the laboratory.

**Changing a rating when the evidence changes isn't backtracking. It's the whole point.**

## About the AI in the Room

There is one other thing I want to be open about from the beginning.

**AI helps me create The Bunker Scale.**

I use it to help research topics, find questions I may have overlooked, organize information, and work through drafts.

That feels appropriate for a publication about AI, but it also makes verification especially important.

The final responsibility for what appears here is human.

Claims that matter will be checked against original research, company disclosures, government documents, credible reporting, or other appropriate sources whenever possible. If something isn't known, I'll say so. If evidence is disputed, I'll try to explain why rather than quietly choosing the version that makes the better headline.

AI can help with the work.

**It does not get to grade its own homework.**

## So, Should We Build the Bunker?

Not yet.

And hopefully never.

But I do think we are living through a period when AI is changing quickly enough that dismissing every concern as fearmongering would be just as unhelpful as assuming every disturbing experiment means disaster is around the corner.

There is a very large space between those two positions.

That's where I intend to spend most of my time.

Looking at what changed.

Figuring out why it matters.

And pointing out what you can probably ignore.
