*Rating as of November 24, 2026: 5/10 · Eyebrow Raised*

*Waymo recalled nearly 4,500 self-driving vehicles after its robotaxis kept driving into freeway lanes that were closed for construction. Nobody was hurt, the cars are off the freeways while the fix is built, and this is the second time this year the same failure has forced a recall.*

## What changed

Alphabet-owned Waymo said on Tuesday, November 24, that it is recalling nearly 4,500 self-driving vehicles in the U.S. [1]. The recall was prompted by 11 incidents since October 1 in which robotaxis entered closed freeway lanes, including three on a single day this month.

Three Waymo vehicles entered freeway lanes marked with cones as closed for construction on November 9, just three days after the company had updated its approved scope of operation to reduce exposure to closed freeway lanes [1].

Waymo said there were no collisions or injuries reported from the closed freeway events, and it is still working on the recall remedy [1].

The recall follows action by California regulators. The California Department of Motor Vehicles barred Waymo vehicles from freeways in the state, calling them an imminent hazard to public safety after a series of incidents in active construction zones, and Waymo has halted operations of its vehicles on U.S. freeways [1].

This is the second recall for the same kind of failure. In June, Waymo issued a similar recall for nearly 3,900 robotaxis that drove past ramp closure signs into pre-planned freeway construction zones or entered freeway lanes with active construction by driving between cones [1].

Waymo also issued a second recall covering how its vehicles respond to manual commands at intersections from law enforcement or other emergency personnel. That one follows a May 8 collision in which a Waymo followed directions from an officer who was then temporarily obstructed from view. That software update was completed on November 6 [1].

## Why it matters

The significance is not a new capability. It is a recurring failure in vehicles already operating in public spaces. The same failure forced a recall in June, the company changed its operating scope to reduce exposure, and three vehicles still entered a closed lane three days later. A fix that does not hold is different from a problem that has never been seen before.

It also matters because the vehicles are not in a test. Waymo runs hundreds of thousands of rides a week, and the closed-lane incidents happened on public freeways, in live traffic, next to construction crews. No one was hurt in these 11 incidents, which is the reason this stops at 5. But the margin between a robotaxi in a closed lane and a worker in that lane is not a margin to rely on.

Regulators are now acting on more than one front. The California freeway ban is in effect, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened a recall query earlier this month after incidents of Waymo vehicles passing stopped school buses, and NHTSA and the National Transportation Safety Board have opened probes after one of Waymo's vehicles struck a child who ran into a street in Santa Monica [1].

## What you can probably ignore

**"Self-driving cars are out of control."** These are specific, repeated failures in specific situations: closed lanes and manual traffic direction. The vehicles continue to operate on city streets, and Waymo has paused freeway operations while the fix is built.

**"The recall means the cars are being taken off the road."** A recall here is a software fix, not a fleet grounding. The freeway pause is Waymo's own decision plus the California ban, not a nationwide shutdown.

**"Eleven incidents means eleven crashes."** There were no collisions or injuries reported from the closed freeway events. The incidents are the cars entering lanes they should not have entered.

## Why it earned this rating

On the side of a higher number:

- A recurring safety failure affecting nearly 4,500 vehicles operating in public spaces, after a similar recall in June [1].
- The failure persisted after the company changed its operating scope to reduce exposure, with three incidents three days later [1].
- Regulators have now acted: a California freeway ban, a NHTSA recall query, and NHTSA and NTSB probes [1].

On the side of a lower number:

- No collisions or injuries were reported from the closed freeway events [1].
- Waymo has halted freeway operations while the remedy is developed, and the manual traffic control update is already complete [1].
- This is a known failure mode with a known fix in progress, not a new capability or an unknown risk.

I put this at **5**. The recurrence and the scale earn attention, and the lack of injuries and the paused freeway operations keep it from going higher.

## What I'm watching next

- **The remedy.** Whether Waymo's fix for closed lanes lands, and whether incidents stop after it does.
- **Whether freeway operations resume,** and what California requires before lifting its ban.
- **The school bus query and the Santa Monica probe,** which are separate from the closed-lane recall and could each move the picture.
- **Whether the same failure shows up again** after the fix, which would say more about the system than any single incident does.
